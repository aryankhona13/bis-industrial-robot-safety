INDUSTRIAL ROBOTS: SAFETY STANDARDS

An advanced technical breakdown of how the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) regulates autonomous systems, sensors, and machine vision in Industry 4.0.

1. The Foundation: Bureau of Indian Standards

What is BIS?

BIS is India's National Standards Body. It develops Indian Standards for products, processes, and services. Its core domains include:

  • Standards & Testing
  • Certification & Conformity Assessment
  • Consumer Protection

What is a Standard?

A standard is a strict set of technical requirements and engineering guidelines that helps make a product or system:

Safe
Reliable
Consistent
Fit for Use

3. Robot Safety: Sensing to Safe Operation

Industrial robots move fast and with high force, creating risks like collision, crushing, and unexpected movement. Here is the step-by-step BIS compliant safety flow:

1. HAZARD

Detect possible danger (collision, crushing, impact).

2. SENSOR

Detects position, proximity, force, or temperature in real-time.

3. CONTROLLER

Processes the safety signal from sensors and instantly decides the response.

4. SAFETY SYSTEM

Controls or completely stops dangerous robot motion.

5. PROTECTIVE DEVICE

Hardware failsafes: Emergency Stop • Physical Guard • Light Curtain • Interlock.

6. TEST & VERIFY ➔ 7. SAFE OPERATION

Check whether the safety function works correctly. The Robot then operates strictly within defined safety conditions.

4. The ECE Connection in Automation

1. Sensor (Detect)

Machine vision cameras, LiDAR, and transducers detect environmental variables and human proximity.

2. Controller (Decide)

Microprocessors process analog/digital signals, calculate pathing, and execute fault-tolerant logic algorithms.

3. Drive/Motor (Act)

Motor drivers convert the controller's electrical energy and PWM signals into precise, heavy mechanical motion.

🚀 Deep Engineering Analytics (Beyond the Poster)

This section outlines the specific engineering theories and strict quantitative limits enforced by BIS and international equivalents that govern the hardware discussed above.

IS 13693 (Safety of Industrial Robots)

Mandates "Collaborative Workspace" limitations. If a human breaches the 1-meter safety radius, the microcontroller must throttle PWM signals to limit the robotic arm speed to below 250 mm/s.

EMI/EMC Shielding Standards

High-power motor drivers generate electromagnetic noise. Standards require opto-isolators and proper PCB grounding so this noise doesn't corrupt the low-voltage sensor data lines (I2C/SPI).

Safe Torque Off (STO) Protocol

An advanced hardware failsafe. Unlike a normal power cut, STO instantly cuts the power-producing pulses to the motor driver without shutting down the main microcontroller, ensuring the system can restart rapidly.

Thermal Runaway Mitigation (IS 16046)

For Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) using lithium battery packs, standard IS 16046 dictates strict Battery Management System (BMS) tolerances for over-voltage and short-circuit thermal protection.
