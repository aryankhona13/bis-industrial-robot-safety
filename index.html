04 • THE MAIN EXAMPLE

Industrial Robots

Powerful automation needs strong safety.

An industrial robot is a programmable machine used to perform manufacturing tasks automatically.

Common applications:

Welding Assembly Handling Painting Inspection Packaging

Robots provide: High Speed • High Precision • Repeatability • Continuous Operation

Automatic movement, high force and unexpected motion can create hazards such as collision, crushing, impact and other mechanical/electrical risks.

08 • BEYOND THE POSTER

Important technical context

Safety is a system problem

A safe robot is not created by one sensor or one emergency button. The robot, tooling, application, environment, control system and protective measures must be considered together.

Risk assessment comes first

The required safeguard depends on the hazard and the way the robot is used. Different applications may need different protective measures.

Functional safety

Some safety functions depend on electrical, electronic or programmable control. The function needs appropriate design, fault consideration and verification.

Robot vs robot cell

A robot is one machine; a robot cell is an integrated application containing the robot, tooling, controls, material and surrounding equipment.

Cybersecurity can matter

When networked or programmable systems affect safety functions, cybersecurity can become relevant to robot safety.

Testing is evidence

Designing a safety function is not the end. Testing and verification provide evidence that the intended safety behaviour is achieved.

Does BIS certify every industrial robot?

No. BIS develops standards and operates conformity-assessment systems. Whether a particular product or application has a mandatory requirement depends on the applicable law, order or scheme.

Why does the poster start with hazards?

Because safety design begins with understanding what can cause harm. Protection should be based on the hazards and risks of the specific application.

Why are sensors not enough?

Sensors only detect conditions. Safe operation also needs appropriate control logic, protective measures, actuation and verification.

Why is testing shown in the flow?

A safety function should be checked against the applicable requirements so that the intended safety behaviour can be verified.
